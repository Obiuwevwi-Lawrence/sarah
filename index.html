About Me

My name is Sarah Halleluyah Adeyemi — a graduate researcher in Bioinformatics at Morgan State University in Baltimore, Maryland, with a 4.0 GPA in my master's program.

I conduct applied analysis of large-scale genomic datasets to identify resistance patterns and emerging trends relevant to public health, and I build predictive machine learning models (Random Forest, SVM) to classify high-risk biological profiles.

My path began in the lab: a B.Sc. in Microbiology from Mountain Top University, Nigeria, followed by research internships in infectious disease at the Nigerian Institute of Medical Research. That mix of wet-lab and computational work shapes how I approach data.

I enjoy translating technical results into clear reports, briefings and presentations, and I'm especially interested in the space where innovation meets public service.

Research Interests

🧬

Antimicrobial Resistance & Machine Learning

End-to-end pipelines — data cleaning, Random Forest/SVM, feature interpretation — to predict resistance and identify genetic biomarkers in E. coli.

📊

Genomics & Data Visualization

Python and R dashboards showing resistance trends and genomic feature importance, built for both researchers and decision-makers.

🔬

Single-Cell RNA-seq

Quality control, normalization, clustering and marker-gene identification for data-driven cell-state interpretation.

🩺

Digital Pathology

Integrating AI, genomics and molecular imaging to advance medical pathology.

🌍

Public Health Surveillance

Large-scale data collection and analysis supporting infectious disease research and antibiotic stewardship.

❤️

Molecular Mechanisms of Stress

Comparative case study on hypoxia in Tetralogy of Fallot, exploring how short-term stress leads to long-term cardiovascular challenges.
